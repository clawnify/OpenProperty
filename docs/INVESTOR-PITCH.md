# KodiTrack: investor pitch and diligence brief

## The honest snapshot

KodiTrack is an early property-operations product aimed at landlords and small property managers. Its current workflow combines property/unit records, tenant and lease details, monthly rent charges, payment receipts, arrears views, lease-scoped overpayment credits, CSV exports, and maintenance work orders.

The product targets a real administrative burden: rental records, payment evidence, follow-ups, and maintenance requests can be scattered across spreadsheets, messaging apps, and paper. KodiTrack's promise is a single, clearer operating record. That is a credible problem to test, not proof of product-market fit.

No verified customer count, revenue, retention, collection improvement, or time-saved figures are available in this repository. Treat KodiTrack as pre-traction until the founder can show customer and usage evidence. Do not present a code feature as an achieved business outcome.

## Short investor pitch (about 90 seconds)

> Independent landlords and small property managers often run rent collection, tenant records, arrears follow-up, and maintenance through a patchwork of spreadsheets, chats, and paper. That makes it difficult to know what is due, what was actually received, and what needs attention.
>
> KodiTrack is an early property-operations workspace that brings those core records together: properties and units, tenants and leases, monthly rent charges, payment receipts, arrears, and maintenance work orders. It records a payment at its full received value, separates the part applied to a rent charge from any surplus, and carries surplus as a lease credit toward the oldest unpaid rent charge. This makes the rent ledger easier to explain and reconcile.
>
> We are not claiming market leadership or proven savings. We are validating whether landlords will pay for a simpler, more reliable operating workflow, starting with a small group of local customers. The next milestones are paid pilots, reliable payment reconciliation, customer retention, and measured reductions in administrative effort. We are looking for support to validate that demand and harden the product against real operational use.

## What is investable—and what is not yet proven

**Potential strengths**

- A specific user and recurring job: keep rent, tenant, lease, and maintenance records coherent.
- Rent receipts and outstanding balances are visible together, rather than being treated as one undifferentiated number.
- A practical starting scope that can be demonstrated to a landlord without claiming to replace a full property-management suite.
- A path to local payment workflows, including a Daraja integration, subject to end-to-end verification and operational controls.

**Key diligence gaps**

- No verified traction evidence is supplied here: paying customers, pipeline conversion, churn, or usage frequency.
- Willingness to pay and target portfolio size need interviews and paid pilots, not assumptions.
- Competition includes spreadsheets, messaging, existing property software, and bookkeeping services. Differentiation and customer acquisition costs are not demonstrated.
- Payment callback behavior, financial reconciliation, production backups, support operations, and security controls need validation before a broad launch.
- The rent ledger is not double-entry accounting, trust accounting, or a tax product. A qualified local professional should review accounting, privacy, consumer-protection, and payment-provider obligations.

## Product assessment

These are qualitative judgments from repository inspection, not market research:

- **Problem relevance:** 7/10. Rent and maintenance coordination is recurring and concrete; the target segment still needs validation.
- **Current product completeness:** 5/10. Useful core workflows exist, but operational reliability, payment reconciliation, tenant-facing flows, and production evidence remain gaps.
- **Differentiation today:** 4/10. Clearer overpayment treatment is useful but not, by itself, a durable competitive moat.
- **Investment readiness today:** 3/10. A product story exists; verified traction, unit economics, reliability evidence, and a defensible go-to-market plan are not established.

**Would I invest today?** Not on the available evidence. I would consider a small, milestone-based pre-seed or pilot conversation after seeing a handful of landlords use the workflow repeatedly and at least some pay for it. I would want proof of recurring use, renewal intent, support burden, payment reconciliation accuracy, and an acquisition channel before pricing a larger investment.

## Proposed validation milestones

1. Interview landlords and managers about their current process, painful exceptions, and existing alternatives. Record findings without leading them toward the product.
2. Run a supervised pilot with a small set of willing customers using test or carefully controlled production workflows.
3. Measure agreed outcomes before and after: time spent on rent follow-up, missing/duplicate records, unresolved maintenance requests, and reconciliation discrepancies.
4. Ask for payment at a clearly stated price. Track conversion, retention, and reasons for rejection.
5. Prove reliable backup/restore, callback retry handling, access isolation, and reconciliation against provider and bank statements.
6. Decide whether the product is best sold to individual landlords, property managers, or a narrower portfolio segment.

## Evidence to bring into an investor meeting

- Named target-customer profile and interview notes, with permission and personal details removed.
- Pilot cohort size, active-use definition, period observed, and retention—only if actually measured.
- Paid conversions, pricing, refunds, and churn, supported by records.
- Before/after operational measurements with methodology and sample size.
- Payment reconciliation test evidence and documented exceptions.
- Hosting, support, compliance, and acquisition-cost assumptions, clearly labeled as estimates until validated.
- Product demo using synthetic data. Do not expose real tenant, payment, or credential information.

## Questions to expect

- Which landlords have paid, and why did others decline?
- How often do customers use the product after setup?
- What is the specific advantage over a spreadsheet and WhatsApp?
- What does the product do when callbacks are delayed, duplicated, or disputed?
- Who handles customer support and reconciliation exceptions?
- What local privacy, tax, and client-money obligations apply?
- How much does it cost to acquire and support a customer?

Answer plainly. If an answer is not known, name the experiment that will establish it.
